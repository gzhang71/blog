I got my California salesperson license a year ago. The whole study plan was
two things: YouTube, and The CE Shop for the required coursework.

The process itself is more sequential than it first looks — most of it is
waiting on a step that can't start until the previous one finishes.

## The order things happen in

| # | Step | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Three college-level courses: Real Estate Principles, Real Estate Practice, and one elective | Each is a minimum of 45 hours; you can't apply for the exam without all three |
| 2 | File the exam application — RE 435 covers exam and license together | Course transcripts must be attached |
| 3 | Live Scan fingerprinting | Can be done in parallel; results go to DRE and the FBI |
| 4 | Schedule the exam through eLicensing | Self-scheduled, subject to seat availability at your center |
| 5 | Sit the exam | 150 multiple-choice questions, 3 hours, four choices each |
| 6 | License issued after DRE approves the application | Only if you filed RE 435; otherwise you apply separately after passing |

Filing RE 435 rather than the exam-only application is the one decision that
saves real calendar time — the license application is already on file when the
exam result lands, instead of starting a second round trip afterward.

Two details about the Real Estate Practice course specifically: since January
2024 it has to include implicit bias and fair housing components, and the fair
housing part requires an interactive segment where you role-play both sides.
Older course material floating around online predates that.

## What it costs

| Item | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Salesperson examination | $100 |
| License | $350 |
| Total with RE 435 | $450 |
| Live Scan fingerprint processing | $49, paid to the provider |
| Rescheduling an exam | $40 first time |

Figures from the [DRE fee schedule](https://www.dre.ca.gov/Licensees/Fees.html)
as of September 2026, and they're nonrefundable. Coursework is separate and on
top of all of this.

## The part worth passing on

The CE Shop's courses expire 180 days after you buy them. The clock starts at
purchase, not on the day you finally sit down and open the first module — so
every week you spend "getting around to it" is a week off the end.

Don't pay until you're actually ready to start. It's tempting to buy early to
commit yourself. That's how you end up with three weeks of access left and
three courses to get through.
